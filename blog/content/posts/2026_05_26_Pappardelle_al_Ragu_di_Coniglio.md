I never intended for this to be a food blog - but here we are.

I hate food blogs that make you scroll through some whole life story - [so here's the recipe](#recipe). It's not a very good recipe - but i'm also not a very good cook, so 🤷‍♀️.

## Mise en Place as Soulcraft

I love cooking. Deeply. The kitchen is truly where my heart comes alive.

A bottle of wine, a slow simmer for hours on end, and no corners cut. I know I only write about code - but cooking is my real soulcraft.

When I look into future I want - it's centered around food. A small plot of land with a big garden. A small house with a big kitchen. A small family with a big table.

Cooking is my love language and there's nothing i love to share more than a meal i've spent all day making. Hearing how much others love my food is what nourishes my soul.

To me - cooking is just steady - it evens my keel. I am constantly dreaming of flavors and textures and ideas for dishes. I hunt for ingredients, but often find better flavors from in-season produce and adjust course. I spend time up front prepping everything. Once everything is in it's right place[^1], it's go time. A performance more artistic than scientific. Full of tiny micro-corrections from the eddy of flavors and smells. A dash of this - a pinch of that - and another pinch - and another - and another. A rare case where precision and creativity shine together. Harmony in chaos.

## A less than amusing-bouche
When I look at my cooking - the slow journey that it is - often starting at the farm down the road - seeing the greens popping through the dark earth and building inspiration from the sights and the smells and the chaos of life - I get this immense joy from the slowness and meandering aspect of it all.

And i think that's why I find AI so unsettling, disappointing, and unfulfilling. There is no mise-en-place. There is no terroir. There are no seasons of growing. There is no curing and storing within the cellar.

It's just all _junk food_. Immediate and acceptable enough. Industrial.

I promise not the dwell on this for too long. But I've spent all day - literally the whole day - preparing for tonight's dinner. And I just feel... deeply satisfied. There's nothing I would trade for the slowness, this wholeness, this deep satisfaction. 

And I've been wondering why I feel so terrible doing such a frankly easy job. And I think this is it. It's just the accumulation of poor health from feeding my soul nothing but junk food at work.

## So what's for dinner, doc?

On the menu tonight is fresh made pappardelle with a rabbit ragu. The ragu is made from white wine and mushroom broth with Chantelle mushrooms, shallots, pearl onions, sweet and tender spring carrots, spring garlic, and fresh marjoram. For the wine - I've picked a floral, slightly mineral yet fruity on the tongue white - an italian verdicchio. At 15 a bottle it's frankly a screaming deal.

It's taken me all day. It's cooking in the oven as we speak. It's probably got at least another hour and a half to go.

> Around 10am - my wife and I mixed the pasta dough. Mostly she mixed and I whined about wishing I had made a note to add extra yolk because the recipe comes out too dry in our crazy high desert climate.

> A bit after noon - I headed out on my bike to get some dried mushrooms and lunch from the french bodega in town. Came back with a few too many french sausages, some fancy mustard, and duck rillettes. Truly a dangerous place to go shopping while hungry.

> Around 2 - I biked over to the grocery store to get shallots, pearl onions, and wine. We already had the spring carrots and garlic on hand from this week's pickup at the farm. i love our [CSA](https://www.speedwellfarmandgardens.com/).

> Around 4 - I rolled out the pasta into thin sheets while my wife cut the sheets it into spectacular ribbons of golden pappardelle.

> Around 5 - I began to prep the veggies. Rehydrate the dried chantrelles in broth, pearl onions flash boiled and peeled, carrots neatly diced, shallots sliced over the mandoline.

> Around 6 - I began to sear the rabbit. A hefty bit of butter to make up for the leanness of the flesh. The out of the pot with the rabbit, and into the pot with the veggies. Caramelize and brown then deglaze with white wine. Mix in the broth, bring the rabbit back to the pot. Into the oven and let time take its course.

> Around 8 - I will take the rabbit out of the pot and shred, while continuing to reduce/thicken the sauce on the stovetop, and finish with another dab of butter plus fresh marjoram and spring garlic greens. Quick boil the pasta. Reintroduce the rabbit back to the sauce to coat.

> around 9 - serve, savor, and be nourished.

## Recipe
I made fresh pasta and I recommend you do too. It really isn't that much work and for a meal like this you'll be glad you did. The dough makes enough pasta for 4 - we eat for 2, so I halve the dough before rolling and keep the remainder in the fridge for another meal later in the week.

### For the pasta
* 350g '00' flour. yes get the '00' - you'll be hooked on this.
* 150g semolina flour
* 1 tsp salt
* ~5 egg yolks (weight your yolks - you're aiming for at least 65g of yolk - which should be about 3 or 4 yolks depending on your eggs - then add 1 more yolk on top of that for a little more richness and moisture). keep the egg whites and make financiers for dessert.[^2]
* 190g whole eggs (easier to portion precisely if you beat them together)

### For the ragu
* One small rabbit - maybe 3 pounds after butchering (I got mine from a local farm but would have preferred to have hunted)
* 3 large shallots (the larger the shallot - the easier to mandolin)
* Roughly half a pound of pearl onions
* 3 fresh spring garlic shoots
* Roughly half a pound of chantrelles or any nice mushrooms you enjoy (I used dried but would have preferred something freshly foraged)
* 3 medium-sized carrots (I used a medley of white, orange, and red carrots from the farm)
* 4 sprigs of fresh marjoram + more leaves for finishing
* A stick of butter - divided in half.
* Half a cup of white wine
* 1 liter of stock (I used mushroom broth we got from the farm but would have preferred homemade chicken stock)
* A teaspoon of flour to thicken the sauce
* A packet of gelatin (optional if you used homemade meat stock)
* Teaspoon of fish sauce
* One ounce toasted hazelnuts
* tablespoon of hazel oil
* Salt to taste

### Making the pasta
* i make my pasta by hand to get it started - then let the stand mixer knead it for me with the dough hook
* combine flours and salt in a bowl and mix with a whisk
* make a little hole (shaped kinda like a volcano) and add the eggs + egg yolks in there
* mix eggs together with a fork, then use a spatula to fold eggs + flour together until the dough is uniformly wet and there's no loose flour left
* if you really love pasta - you can knead by hand on your counter top. i move it to my stand mixer at this point and let it knead on slow speed for ~8 mins
* wrap your pasta in a tight plastic film and set aside for at least 4 hours.
* i use a machine to roll my pasta into sheets and go as thin as i can. with pappardelle being such a wide noodle, i find that the thinner it is the better the texture will be.
* cut the sheets into ~1 inch ribbons and hang to air dry for at least an hour.

### Making the ragu
* I started by prepping my stock + mushrooms - but you can skip/adjust depending on what you're working with. Brought the stock to a simmer and added the mushrooms. Let the mushrooms hydrate for 15 minutes at simmer then strained out and pressed the juices out of. Put stock in a thin metal bowl to cool. Once cooled I added a packet of gelatin to thicken. Trust me - you want a thick sauce.
* slice the shallots with a mandolin[^3] and set aside.
* thinly dice the carrots and set aside.
* Thinly slice the white bulb of the spring garlic and set aside. The greens should also be sliced but set aside separately to finish with later.
* Prep the pearl onions by slicing the tops and bottoms off and cutting a thin X across the top/bottom.
* Get a small pot of water to a rolling boil.
* Flash boil the pearl onions about a minute and a half (maybe less if you're not at 5500ft of altitude - our water only hits 202 degrees here!) then blanch in cold water.
* Peel the outer layer off the pearl onions and set aside.
* Add half a stick of butter and start heating the dutch oven on the stovetop over medium-high heat.
* Once the butter is sizzling add the rabbit and let sear. Be careful not to scorch the butter and adjust heat as needed. After 5 minutes - the rabbit should be nicely browned. Flip and give it another 5 minutes on the other side. its ok if a little meat sticks to the pot - that'll just add flavor when we deglaze.
* Take the rabbit out of the dutch oven and set aside.
* Preheat oven to 300.
* Add shallots + carrots to dutch oven and cook over medium high heat until just beginning to brown. Throw in a couple nice pinches of salt.
* Add the pearl onions, mushrooms, and garlic whites and cook until liquid has cooked out and things are beginning to stick to the bottom of the dutch oven.
* Mix in the flour and let cook until fragrant (maybe 30 seconds).
* Deglaze with white wine and continue to cook until the liquid just about fully cooked out.
* Add the stock and bring to a simmer. Add the sprigs of fresh marjoram and submerge the rabbit as much as you can. Put the dutch oven into the oven and let braise - lid slightly ajar.
* After about 1 hour flip the rabbit (I was using a whole rabbit - if you have pieces of rabbit then you're probably ok) and put the dutch oven back in the oven for another hour.
* Pull the dutch oven out of the oven and put on stovetop. Remove the rabbit from the pot. Set aside to cool until ready to handle.
* Turn the heat up on the dutch oven until simmering then reduce heat to maintain. Add the remaining fresh marjoram leaves, spring garlic greens, and teaspoon of fish sauce. Be sure to salt to taste at this point. 
* Add remaining half a stick of butter to sauce. Let sauce simmer for a bit. The goal here is to reduce until you have a rich sauce that will stick to your noodles. It took about 20 minutes for me.
* Meanwhile, when the rabbit is cool shred the meat off the bones. It should be quite easy to do at this point as the meat should be tender and more or less fall off the bone.
* Once you're satisfied with the thickness of the sauce, take off heat and add the rabbit meat back in. Set aside and cook your pasta now.

### Cooking the pasta
* Get roughly 2 liters of water to a rolling boiling in a large pot.
* Add more salt to the water than you think is necessary. And then a little more after that. Fresh pasta needs more salt than dried.
* Once at a rolling boil - add your pappardelle and keep the heat high.
* Cook until al dente - this took roughly 8 minutes for me at 5500ft altitude. Probably less for you.

### Plating
* Generously portion out pappardelle between 3 and 4 plates (or better yet - bowls if you like it exxxtra sauce-y)
* Even more generously add the ragu over the pasta. I like mine to be basically swimming in sauce. Your tastes may vary.
* Mash up your toasted hazelnuts with a mortar/pestal and sprinkle on top. Drizzle with hazel oil.
* Dust the love out of your wets.[^4]
* Some fresh parsley might be nice too.
